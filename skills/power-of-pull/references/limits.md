# Limits of the Model

Read this before giving a verdict. Applying PULL without knowing where it is weak produces confident bad advice.

## 1. The evidence base is anecdotal

The method comes from the author's own companies, the founders he advised and the sales calls he analysed. The case studies are mostly B2B startups that reached roughly $1M in revenue. There is no controlled study. Treat it as a well argued practitioner method, not an empirical finding.

## 2. It is strongest for B2B, early stage and considered purchases

One to one selling, sales as research and the four part call assume a buyer you can talk to. For mass consumer products, impulse purchases or marketplaces with thousands of tiny buyers, the logic (demand comes from what is on the buyer's list) still holds, but the tactics translate poorly. Say so rather than forcing a sales call script onto a consumer app.

## 3. It assumes the buyer has a choice

In captive markets, commodities, mandated procurement or long contractual tenders, "weird not to buy" is shaped by rules and procurement more than by PULL. The framework can still explain why one stakeholder champions a purchase, but not the whole decision.

## 4. Bottom up can trap you in a small niche

Designing for one hell yes customer is the right start, but it can lead to a business that only ever serves a narrow situation. The book's answer is that businesses unfold step by step; the honest caveat is that some niches stay niches. Flag it when the observed PULL depends on a rare trigger.

## 5. Selling early needs something to sell

Selling a manual or service version first works for many B2B offers. It works less well for deep technology, regulated products or anything that cannot be delivered by hand. Say when the minimum viable supply genuinely needs building before it can be sold.

## 6. Internal initiatives bend the model

Applied inside a company, "buyers" are stakeholders with budgets, politics and mandates. PULL helps find the stakeholder whose project is truly unavoidable, but it does not remove the need for executive sponsorship.
