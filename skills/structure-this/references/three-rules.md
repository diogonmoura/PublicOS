# The three rules of a pyramid

## The rule

The mind groups ideas automatically and cannot hold more than about seven loose
items at once. A document that hands the reader an unsorted pile forces them to
build their own structure — usually not the one you intended. This is *why* the
pyramid exists, not a stylistic preference layered on top of it.

A valid pyramid obeys three rules, all the time, at every level:

1. **Every idea summarises the ideas grouped beneath it.** A parent node is not a
   label for its children — it is a conclusion drawn from them.
2. **The ideas in each group are of the same logical kind.** Do not group a
   problem with a recommendation, or a cause with a symptom.
3. **The ideas in each group are in an explicit logical order.** Not "here are
   some things" — a stated, defensible order (see `orders-and-mece.md`).

## How to test it

Run the vertical Q&A test on any node in the structure:

1. Read the node in isolation.
2. Name the exact question it raises in the reader's mind.
3. Look at the level directly below it. Confirm it answers *that* question — not
   a related question, not a broader one, not one the writer wished had been
   raised.
4. If the children answer a different question, either rewrite the parent to
   match what they actually answer, or replace the children with ones that
   answer the question the parent raises.

Do this for every parent node, not just the top. A pyramid can pass at level one
and fail at level three.

## Failure modes

- **Mismatched question.** A node says "We should expand to two new markets" and
  its children explain *why the current market is saturated* — that answers "why
  should we not stay put," not "which two markets and why."
- **Mixed kind.** A group contains two problems and one recommendation. The
  reader cannot tell if the group is diagnosing or prescribing.
- **No stated order.** A bullet list of "things to consider" with no temporal,
  structural, or comparative order — items could be shuffled with no loss of
  meaning, which means the grouping isn't really a grouping.
