# Deduction vs. induction

## The rule

Ideas at the same level of the pyramid relate to each other in one of two ways.

- **Deduction** chains: an assertion, a comment on that assertion, and the
  implication of the two together (X is the case; X implies Y; therefore Z).
  It forces the reader to carry each step's context forward into the next one,
  which is tiring — lose the thread at step two and step three makes no sense.
- **Induction** groups ideas of the same class under a plural noun (three
  reasons, three risks, three regions). The reader can take each item
  independently, in any order, without carrying context between them. It reads
  far faster.

Recommendation: use induction at the upper levels of the pyramid, where the
reader is skimming for the shape of the argument. Reserve deduction for the
low levels, where the reader has already bought into the branch and is willing
to follow a chain of reasoning in detail.

## How to test it

For any group of sibling nodes:

1. Read them as a chain: does node 2 only make sense given node 1, and node 3
   only given 1 and 2? If children read as "X is the case" / "if X, then Y" /
   "therefore Z," the branch is deductive.
2. Try to name the plural noun that would cover the group as a set (three
   reasons, four risks, two options). If a plural noun fits cleanly and each
   child stands on its own without the others, the branch is inductive.
3. If neither test resolves cleanly, the group is probably deductive dressed up
   with bullet points — check for hidden dependencies between the items before
   calling it inductive.

## Failure modes

- **A top-level branch built deductively.** This is where readers stop
  reading — the top of the pyramid is exactly where the reader is deciding
  whether to keep going, and a chain they must follow step-by-step to extract
  meaning loses them fastest.
- **A vague plural noun that covers anything.** "Factors," "aspects,"
  "elements" — if the label is this generic, the group usually isn't a real
  induction; it's a deductive chain, or an unrelated pile, wearing an inductive
  label to look tidy.
