# Orders and MECE

## The rule

Every legitimate group of ideas is ordered by exactly one of three principles.
There is no fourth.

- **Temporal** — the steps of a process, in the order they happen. First do X,
  then Y, then Z.
- **Structural** — the parts of a whole. This is the only order of the three
  that requires MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive): the parts
  must not overlap, and together they must account for the whole thing being
  divided.
- **Comparative** — items ranked by degree of importance, strongest or most
  significant first.

If a group fits none of the three, the grouping is wrong. This is a defect in
the thinking, not in the formatting — reordering the bullets will not fix it.
The fix is to re-think what is actually being grouped, and often to redraw the
group's boundaries entirely.

## How to test it

For every group in the pyramid:

1. Name its order out loud, in one word: temporal, structural, or comparative.
   If you hesitate or reach for a fourth word ("logical," "thematic"), the
   group has not been thought through.
2. If the order is structural, state explicitly what whole is being divided
   (the market, the budget, the causes of the outage) and confirm the parts:
   - do not overlap (mutually exclusive)
   - together cover the whole (collectively exhaustive)
3. If neither a temporal, structural, nor comparative reading survives, stop —
   do not force a label onto the group. Re-derive it from the parent's
   question instead.

## Failure modes

- A group labelled "structural" whose parts secretly overlap (e.g. "cost
  issues" and "budget issues" as separate bullets under the same whole).
- A group forced into an order it doesn't have — three unrelated observations
  wearing a fake "comparative" label because nobody checked.
- Treating MECE as the whole method. It applies to structural groups only, not
  to temporal or comparative ones — don't run the exclusivity/exhaustiveness
  check where it doesn't belong.

> MECE is asserted and checked, not belaboured. Running a full MECE proof on a
> three-bullet email is the characteristic failure of this method — it
> consumes disproportionate time on small problems. Check it; do not litigate
> it.
